LOCKSTEP OWN-SHIP PREDICTION (roadmap A2: hide the input delay)
1 November 2026 | offline implementation and unit tests; no X4 launched, no live result yet

SUMMARY

Goal (user): on the own screen the own ship answers the controls at once, while the shared simulation
applies the same inputs d steps later underneath; the other players' ships are drawn where they are
expected to be and corrected smoothly as their steps arrive. The prediction is display only; the census
(about 530,000 objects compared between clients) must stay identical.

Delivered on contrib/ferrite-predict2 (base: the smoke e457b4c9), default off, nothing changes without
native_gate_own_predict:
- src/native/engine/lockstep_own_predict.h: the predictor for stock piloting (the people config's path):
  per slot an online-fitted command model (stock record -> X4's processed control channels) and response
  model (channels -> body rates, acceleration limited), a prediction from the newest authoritative pose
  every step, the correction policy of section 3, error measurement against the authoritative pose,
  and the slot-10 display (a scoped pointer swap of the ship's published pose block, every byte back
  before slot 10 returns). Stop reason 184 (present failures only).
- src/native/engine/lockstep_own_predict_lua.h: gate.own_predict / own_predict_frame / own_predict_status.
- lockstep_pilot_predict.h: the existing slot-10 call pair (predict_present_enter/leave, called by the
  render split's wrapper) now serves both presenters; lockstep_render_rng.h is untouched (the render
  frames branch edits it).
- tools/dev/native_gate_probe.lua: arming per client, an OWN_PREDICT row per step written at once,
  OWN_PREDICT_STATUS every 600 ticks, OWN_PREDICT_STOP. tools/dev/check_gate_options.py: the rules.
- Tests: src/native/engine/test_lockstep_own_predict.h (in test_lockstep_gate.exe),
  tests/test_native_gate_own_predict.py.
What works offline: everything above on fake engine memory and a synthetic ship (section 9). Not verified:
anything about the real engine (the layouts read are the ones lockstep_pilot_present.h and
lockstep_pilot_eye.h already use live, but the prediction quality on X4's real flight model, the census
with present on, and the look are open). The live-test spec is section 11.

1. WHAT "HIDING THE DELAY" MEANS UNDER LOCKSTEP

A record captured at tick c applies at c+d on every client (d = native_gate_pilot_delay: 3 ticks = 100 ms
at 30 steps/s with lookahead 1; lookahead 3 + delay 5 = 167 ms, the 1 Nov latency test at 100 ms round
trip). The simulated ship S(t) is therefore the latency-free ship N(t) shifted by d: N(T) = S(T+d). Showing
an estimate of S(T+d) in the frame of tick T is exactly the native, delay-free motion of the own ship; the
rest of the world stays at T (the "client a little ahead" of reports/LOCKSTEP-PREDICTION-20261029.txt).
Every record that decides S(T+1..T+d) has been captured on this client by tick T: the own slot's
prediction knows all of its inputs. Other players' records reach this client after the relay round
trip; at tick T those applying up to about T+d-(round trip in steps) are known, the rest are guessed.

Why a new module: lockstep_pilot_predict.h (29 Oct) predicts from pilot records (Q15 targets for the
Agent's Steering). The people config flies with stock piloting (lockstep_pilot_stock.h: X4's own
PlayerController path, replayed from each client's recorded dispatch stream of control ids, cursor
outputs and SPACE edges), whose records carry no targets, and the option checker refuses the old
predictor on stock clients. The new module reuses its rotation math, the view-cache pins and the slot-10
call pair; the old predictor is unchanged and the two are exclusive (arming refuses).

2. DESIGN

2.1 Read every step (MoveWorker, VBI slot 6, after this tick's stock capture; guarded reads, no writes)
- Pose S(T): the ship's published view-cache pose block [A] (object+0xe0+A*0x80: +0 cached parent, +0x28
  the local PosRot, row 0 the translation, A = *0x38f4ee0 on this thread). This is exactly what slot 10
  draws; the parent must be the live parent (+0x70).
- Controls u(T): the slot's processed channels after this tick's update (input bank, report
  LOCKSTEP-VIRTUAL-PLAYERS-RE section 2): yaw/pitch/roll 0x2f4b750..758, smoothed throttle 0x2f4b764,
  x/y/z 0x2f4b788..790, flight control 0x2f4b828. Slot 1 from the canonical bank (the probe's frame runs
  with the presentation scope paused), slots 2..N from their stores (lockstep_pilot_slots.h order).
- Inputs: the stock record applied at T (staging ring; a consumed entry keeps its bytes), and the
  pending ones applying at T+1..T+h: the own slot's from this client's capture ring (pstk_locals), other
  slots' from the staging ring once they have arrived. Records are decoded with pstk_decode.

2.2 Command model (records -> u)
Features phi(t): the record's three cursor outputs (mouse steering; zero without the cursor flag), the
held values of the seven replayed ranges (stick axes, throttle axis; the wheel 0xa is an impulse of its
own tick), the held states of the 26 replayed states (keys), folded from the event stream in order.
Model per channel: u(t) = W [u(t-1), phi(t), 1], multi-output recursive least squares (forgetting 0.999,
no forgetting while the covariance trace is above 1e7, so no wind-up without excitation). This form holds
static maps (mouse and stick), key ramps (a lag) and integrators (the throttle setpoint under the
accelerate/decelerate keys). A channel uses its model after 30 samples and only while its one-step error
(slow mean) is below 0.9x the error of holding the last value; a predicted value is clamped to the range
seen plus a tenth. Otherwise it holds.

2.3 Response model (u -> body rates)
Body rates as lockstep_pilot_predict.h: linear x/y/z and angular x/y/z in the previous pose's frame, by
finite difference. Per axis v(t) = a v(t-1) + b.u(t) + c (RLS, prior: constant velocity), i.e. a first
order response towards a target linear in the controls. X4 limits the acceleration, which a linear model
cannot express, so: a step change at 0.8x the envelope of changes or more (rise and fall kept apart) is
the limit, not fitted (fitting it as linear drags the whole fit; seen in the synthetic tests), and a
predicted change is clamped to the envelope. The envelope grows by half at most per step once
established (one impulse cannot open it) and decays slowly (0.9999 per step). A step whose prediction
error is beyond 4 sigma, leaves more than half of the change unexplained and comes with no control change
is an impulse (a hit, a ram, an explosion): not fitted, unless refusals become frequent (above 12 % in the
fast mean: then the model is the wrong one and fits again). An axis uses its model after 30 samples and
only while its one-step error (slow mean, of the clamped prediction) is below 0.9x the error of constant
velocity: never knowingly worse than dead reckoning. Flight control off: constant velocity.

2.4 Prediction
Every step from the newest authoritative S(T) and v(T): commands u(T+1..T+h) from the pending records (a
missing one holds the previous controls and cursor), then velocities and poses. h = min(d, cap), cap
native_gate_own_predict_horizon (default 10 steps = 333 ms). A model error never accumulates beyond one
horizon. The measured full-horizon prediction P(T+h) is kept with three baselines for comparison when T+h
is simulated: constant velocity from S(T) ("dead"), no prediction at all (S(T), "none": what the delay
costs today), and the displayed pose ("display").

2.5 Other players' ships
Same model per slot, fitted on every client from the same authoritative ticks; predicted to the same
T+h (where their owners see them, consistent with the own ship: every player ship is drawn d steps ahead
of the world). Known records drive the first steps, the remaining ones hold the last known controls; when
the real records arrive, the next step's prediction differs and the correction (3) takes the jump.
native_gate_own_predict_others = false predicts the own ship only.

3. CORRECTION POLICY (answers the coordinator's question; constants in lockstep_own_predict.h)

The display is D(T) = P(T+h_eff) composed with a correction offset C. Every step:
- smooth   C takes the jump between the previous display path continued to this step (its end state
           advanced on the newest command) and the new prediction, and decays with exp(-dt/tau),
           tau = native_gate_own_predict_tau (default 150 ms, 0..2000). A new own input causes no jump
           (the continuation runs on it). The part of a jump inside a band of 5 cm plus 10 % of the
           step's motion (2 mrad plus 10 % of its rotation) is shown at once: the steady update of an
           accelerating ship is no lag (a 2 % band gave a display error several times the model's on
           the synthetic ship; with 10 % it is within 15 % of it: 0.0165 against 0.0143 m).
- snap     C beyond 20 m plus one step of the ship's own motion, or beyond 0.35 rad (20 degrees), is
           dropped: the display jumps to the prediction (counted: snaps).
- reset    a discontinuity clears the history, the models stay: D = S(T), the lead grows back over 2h
           steps (counted by cause): the ship changed (respawn: pilot_slots object), the parent changed
           (docking, undocking, a zone or highway change), a step 50 m off the ship's own previous
           velocity (teleport, jump), an unreadable pose, a dead slot.
- lead     h_eff moves towards its target by half a step per step: target 0 at travel speed (above
           1200 m/s, back below 800 m/s) and while the predicted lead exceeds 300 m or 0.6 rad, else h.
           So entering travel drive or a highway the lead shrinks over 2h steps instead of snapping, and
           grows back after it.
- horizon  capped at native_gate_own_predict_horizon steps; a delay above the cap is only partly hidden.
- camera   the cockpit camera hangs under the ship (cockpit view: the render composes the world relative
           to the cockpit through the ship's pose block), so it follows D, which is continuous except at a
           snap or a reset; the correction decays at tau, so a correction never jerks the view.
The cases asked about:
  collisions, rams, hits, explosions: the impulse is in S(T+1) at once; the next prediction starts from it
    (no accumulated error), the response fit refuses the step, the display converges over tau (snap only
    if the path moved more than 20 m plus a step: a large explosion). Measured offline: a 1.5 m path jump
    decays below 5 % within 40 steps (tau 150 ms, 60 steps/s), a 50 m jump snaps once and is not a reset.
  docking / undocking: the ship's parent changes (station/dock): reset; while docked the ship does not
    move, the lead is 0.
  travel drive, highways: the lead ramps to 0 (speed rule); a zone or highway change is a parent change
    (reset); a velocity step of 50 m in one step is a reset.
  teleports, respawn: reset (jump / ship cause); a dead slot shows nothing predicted until it respawns.
  other players' inputs inside the delay window: held, then corrected over tau when they arrive (the
    "missing" count per row says how many of the h records were guessed).
  flight control off (menus, map, autopilot): constant velocity, the lead stays (the ship keeps flying).
  aiming and firing: section 6.

4. DISPLAY TODAY (one frame per step) AND WHY THE CENSUS CANNOT SEE IT

'present' (needs native_gate_render_rng_split = true): the render split's wrapper calls
predict_present_enter() before slot 10 (GameRender::Move: every camera, the HUD draw) and
predict_present_leave() in its __finally. There, on the MoveWorker only, for each predicted ship whose
published block [A] still holds the sampled S(T) (1 cm, 1e-4) under the sampled parent: a private 16-byte
aligned copy of the block (the 0x60 bytes: PosRot with its w lanes, then the scale and velocity words as
the engine has them) with the displayed xyz rows; the entry [A]+0x28 is swapped to it by compare-and-swap
and swapped back by compare-and-swap at the leave (an engine pointer that replaced ours stands: counted
lost). This is lockstep_pilot_eye.h's published-pose technique, used live by the people config on the
player's own block; here on the ships' blocks. The render walk (0x3de630) composes every object relative
to the camera's cockpit through these blocks, so the cockpit view shows the world from the predicted own
ship and the other players' ships at their predicted poses.
Census argument: the module writes no engine memory outside slot 10; in slot 10 it changes one pointer
per predicted ship in the bank only the MoveWorker reads during slot 10 (other threads read the other
index), and puts it back before slot 10 returns, also on an exception. It draws no RNG and calls no engine
code. What remains is what any client-local camera already exercises: slot-10 consumers of the drawn pose
(LOD flag, motion-vector history, render-side welding effects, camera-distance effects; listed in the 29
Oct report section 3), which the render split, native_gate_runtime_ids_local and the view fixes of the
people config already isolate for per-client cameras. The census run of section 11 decides; 'swap' mode
(the swap with the simulated pose itself) separates a swap effect from a pose effect if it differs.
Precondition checks per call (fail closed per ship, counted): stale tick, another thread, block not S(T)
(and not the previous step's, which a render-only frame on the other bank may show), parent changed,
unreadable block, failed swap. A nested entry or a failed restore stops the gate (184).

5. THE RENDER-FRAMES JOIN (lockstep_render_frames.h, contrib/ferrite-render-frames)

Render-only frames run slot 10 only (no slot 6: no new step for this module), so between two steps the
same predictions are presented again. The interface (all in lockstep_own_predict.h; no file of the render
branch was edited):
- double (*opred_display_offset)(void): set by the render module at its arm. Returns the display time of
  the current slot 10 relative to this step, in steps, -1..1 (0 = the step itself, e.g. 0.5 half way to
  the next step; -1+alpha if the interpolator draws the world between T-1 and T). The module advances the
  displayed pose by that offset at its last rates and decays the correction by it. NULL (today): 0.
- int opred_owns(const void *object): 1 while slot 10 draws that object at a predicted pose. The
  interpolator runs inside predict_present_enter/leave (after the enter, before the leave) and leaves
  these objects alone; outside that window it must skip the predicted slot ships itself.
- int opred_display_pose(unsigned slot, double offset, float out[12]): a slot's displayed pose (zone
  frame: position and the three axes) at any offset, for a module that wants to draw it itself.
- The block check accepts S(T) or the previous step's pose (the other bank after a publish), counted as
  present_previous.
The prediction itself, its correction policy and the logs are the same with or without render frames.

6. AIMING AND FIRING

- Fire rides the record (PSTK_FLAG_FIRE / FIRE2) and applies at T+d: when the simulated ship reaches the
  pose the player saw when pressing (up to the prediction error). The shot leaves from there.
- Aim is recorded in the ship's frame (lockstep_pilot_stock.h): with nothing picked it is the cursor ray,
  the same in the predicted and the simulated view (the camera sits in the cockpit), so the shot goes
  where the player pointed relative to the ship. A picked point comes from X4's camera pick (717ed0, run
  in UniverseFrameUpdate on the simulated camera), so it is off by the lead (about lead_pos / range plus
  lead angle). Fix designed, not done: run the pick with the ship's live pose predicted.
- Projectiles, impacts and NPCs are simulated and drawn at T; the own ship and the other players' ships
  at T+d. A shot therefore appears d steps after the trigger, at the simulated muzzle (behind the drawn
  ship by its lead). Against another player, aiming at the drawn (extrapolated) ship aims where the
  simulation will have it when the shot is fired; against an NPC (drawn at T) the shot fires d steps
  later. A shot can look like a hit and miss in the simulation (or the reverse); the simulation, and with
  native_gate_hit_claims = 'victim' the victim's own client, stays authoritative.
- HUD brackets, lead marks and distances are computed in slot 6 from the simulated state: off by the lead
  while flying fast or turning (a rotation lead of 100 ms at 40 deg/s is 4 degrees). Mitigations designed
  in the 29 Oct report (an offset export for the HUD Lua; the camera-table rewrite); not done.

  X4LAN_CONFIG.native_gate_own_predict_rows = 1        -- an OWN_PREDICT row every N steps (0 none)
Needs stock piloting with this client capturing its own slot (capture source 'stock'); 'present' and

native_gate_pilot_predict. Probe refusals happen before loading; the gate's reason is in the error row.

8. LOGS (never hashed, never in the declared state)

OWN_PREDICT_ARMED slot= mode= others= tau_ms= horizon_cap= rows=
OWN_PREDICT tick=T h=H sK=h_eff/err_pos/err_ang/lead_pos/lead_ang/snaps/missing per predicted slot, written
  at once every native_gate_own_predict_rows steps:
    h_eff      the shown horizon (0 while travel or after a reset, H in flight)
    err_pos/ang the error (m, rad) of the prediction made h steps ago against the authoritative pose that
               landed now ('-' when none landed)
    lead_pos/ang how far the shown pose is from the authoritative one now (m, rad): the visible lead
    snaps      total snaps; missing: records guessed in this step's horizon
  sK=- : nothing predicted this step (dead slot, unreadable pose).
OWN_PREDICT_STATUS (every 600 ticks) / OWN_PREDICT_STOP: mode, horizon, cap, delay, tau, frames, stage,
  failures, present_calls/applied/restored/lost/refused/stale/previous/thread; per slot: own, samples,
  predictions, checked, h_eff, snaps, bounded, suspends, missing, actions, edges, nonfinite,
  resets=read:,parent:,jump:,ship:,dead:, ready=<hex> (bits 0..6 command channels, 8..13 axes on their
  model), command= and axes= sample counts, refused= per axis, model=/dead=/none=/display= position
  mean/rms/max,angle mean/rms/max, lead= the same for the shown lead, hist= model position error
  histogram (edges 0.01, 0.03, 0.1, 0.3, 1, 3, 10 m).

10. RISKS AND OPEN POINTS

- The real flight model: X4's control scheme (d6d420) and solver (d93440) are not of the model's form
  (assist, roll mix, response curves, boost, travel). The model only replaces hold/constant velocity
  where it measures better; how much better on X4 is what O1 measures. RAW rows like the 29 Oct ones are
  not added; the status carries the fit state (ready mask, sample counts, refusals).
- Pose block [A] in slot 6 = what slot 10 draws: assumed from the 29 Oct static analysis and the live
  present/eye scope; if slot 6 sees the previous step's block, everything is consistently one step
  behind (the horizon is then short by one; O1's err rows show it as a constant lead error).
- Velocity noise: finite differences of float32 zone positions (about 0.06 m/s at 20 km); heavy noise
  (0.3 m/s in the Python replica of the fit) degrades the forward-axis fit. Far from a zone's origin the
  linear model may stay on constant velocity.
- Stage-1 artefacts (29 Oct report): HUD brackets and lead marks, frustum culling and LOD (camera table,
  zone space), world-space effects stay simulated; the camera pick is simulated (section 6).
- Slot-10 consumers that read the drawn pose and reach the simulation: none known beyond those the people
  config already isolates; the census decides (P1). 'swap' separates the swap from the pose.
- A presenting client (slot k >= 2) draws in the presentation scope; the ship swap happens before the
  scope opens (predict_present_enter precedes pres_render_call), so slot k's ship is swapped as any other.
- The thresholds (band, snap, travel speeds, lead bounds) are first guesses; O1/P2 rows give the numbers
  to tune them.
