0. RESULT
  Today (smoke 08f4690f) nobody can walk in a lockstep session: the GET_UP key is refused everywhere, and the docked menu's
  Get Up button was not guarded at all: it ran X4's GetUp on the clicking client only (a split, then stop 155). Walking keys
  were "presentation" and would have moved the canonical player on whichever client pressed them.
  Built (default off, native_gate_pilot_foot, stop reason 185): slot 1 gets up and walks with X4's own first-person path on
  every client from slot 1's recorded input, like it flies; the docked menu's GetUp is guarded (every session with the guard)
  and with foot claimed into slot 1's record; slot 1's walking state is in the receipt; client k keeps its own eye at its own
  seat while slot 1 walks. Hiring: the conversation family now treats the map's Hire button as one scope: slot 1's fee and
  its conversation answer are replayed together on every client; slots 2+ are refused as a whole (no fee without the hire).
  Not yet: slots 2..N on foot, seeing another player's body, sitting down again / talking to a person / consoles through the
  camera pick, transporters. Sections 1-3 name every gap, 5 the live specs (no people), 6 the people checklist.

4a The flow [F scripts]. On a station a person who can be hired is an instanced NPC (md/npc_instantiation.xml creates them
  with the station's attention and sets $HiringFee: CalculateNPCHiringFee, HiringFeeFromCombinedSkill). No stock Lua starts
  a walk-up talk: the engine starts conversation 'default' with the person [I: through the pick's interaction or the interact
  menu; the interact menu's Comm talks to control entities only, menu_interactmenu.lua buttonComm]. md/conversations.xml
  DefaultComm: for a person who is not a control entity, whose role is hirable, whose role object is a station and who has a
  $HiringFee, the choice 'Hire (fee)' at top_left (section g_hireworksomewhere). SectionHandler opens the map
  (open_conversation_menu MapMenu, mode 'hire'): the player picks a target (own ship or station) and a role or post
  (GetSuitableControlPosts, AssignHiredActor with checkonly as a query). buttonHire: GetPlayerMoney check, then
  TransferPlayerMoneyTo(fee, the person's container) (Lua global 0x2881f0: the only payment), then
  ProceedFromMenu('g_hire', {target, role, ispost}); SectionHandler g_hire: assign_hired_actor (post or role) ->
  event_player_assigned_hired_actor -> NPCAssigned (after the conversation ends) -> NPCAssigned_Transfer (set_owner player,
  intransit, assign_control_entity or set_entity_role + create_npc_template, info_updatePeople). The fee is $HiringFee times
  the container's hiring discounts. No room or distance check gates any of it (the greeting's line checks isinspeakrange).

  is the pilot promotion only (menu_playerinfo.lua). Dialog buttons: dialogmenu.lua positions 315/270/225/45/90/135 degrees

15j-b The mechanism [F, pinned image]
  FindSpaceInRangeAction (vtable 0x2c0f660; action 0x750 find_cluster_in_range, and find_sector_in_range) Execute 0xb8a890
  collects the spaces within the jump range with 0x3bb200 (clusters; BFS over the gates) or 0x78cc30 (sectors) into a
  red-black tree on its stack (rbp-0x79). The tree is keyed by the Component* VALUE: map[] 0x3135a0, insert position 0x345700
  `cmp r8,[rbx+0x20]; jae`. Execute then erases the spaces its match conditions refuse, and at 0xb8af09 calls 0xc602e0
  (declaration, tree, exec). 0xc602e0's only store, 0xd2ada0 (only caller 0xc604d8), walks the tree in order: it appends every
  space (multiple) or takes the Random::U64(count)-th one (single). 0xc602e0 has one caller, so this is the whole range-find
  family. Layout: tree +0x20 first node, +0x28 count, the walk ends at the tree object; node +0 parent, +8 left, +0x10
  right, +0x20 Component*, +0x28 int jumps. FIND_ORDER (lockstep_find_order.h) covered only FindComponentAction's vector
  (0xb87ae3), so these lists were never sorted and no row showed them.
  [I] The clusters' relative addresses come from the load's allocations. If another thread's allocations interleave
  differently, one cluster object can land at another address and reorder the list. That would make the split rare: only
  d1s3's client 1 of the 8 clients read here shows another order. The two GM generations of the run (ticks 1 and 7) are
  its earliest range finds whose result reaches the world.
