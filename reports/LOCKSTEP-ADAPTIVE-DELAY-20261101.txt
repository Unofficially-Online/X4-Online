LOCKSTEP ADAPTIVE INPUT DELAY (ROADMAP A3): MEASURED ROUND TRIPS, A PLAN AT TICK 0, AGREED CHANGES, A STALL BOUND
1 November 2026 | offline work on branch contrib/ferrite-adelay (base: smoke e457b4c9). No X4 was launched and no lane tool was
run. Evidence: C unit tests (test_gate.exe), Python/LuaJIT unit tests, a discrete-event model of the native client against the
real relay code, and the real relay process over loopback UDP. "live" marks what only a native run can show.

0. PROBLEM AND RESULT
  Lookahead k (relay --lookahead, native_gate_lookahead) and the pilot delay (>= k+2) are fixed per session config today. A
  session far from its relay is slow with k too small; one next to it pays input delay it does not need with k too large. Live
  1 Nov (relay --latency-ms 100, 2 clients, 30 steps/s): k=1 11.3 steps/s (0.38x), k=3 + delay 5 22.8 (0.76x), 27.3 without
  latency.
  Built (all default off; a new protocol 5 on the existing wire, protocols 1-4 byte-identical):
  1. Measurement: the relay pings every client (NGPING/NGPONG) and logs each one's round trip (min/p50/p90, in the report).
  2. Plan at tick 0: the relay holds tick 0 until it has 8 round trips per client, picks k from the slowest client's p90 round
     trip and the target step rate, and announces k and the pilot delay in every permit from permit 1 on. Clients check it
     against their maximum, adopt the pilot delay before the first gated step and fail closed on any disagreement.
  3. Changes during a session (--adaptive-changes): an agreed schedule 'from proposal tick c the input lag is L', announced
     before any client sealed a tick it changes; raising leaves gap permits, lowering makes quiet ticks. Proven exact in the
     model under jitter, spikes, 2% loss and mixed clients: every input exactly once, in order, never late.
  4. Stalls: a client the others waited for 1 s is reported (NATIVE_GATE STALL); opt-in, past a second bound it is dropped and
     the others go on with identical inputs. Slot-bound inputs (its ship's pilot records) still stop the session until drop and
     rejoin (roadmap D15) exist; section 4 says what that needs.
  Side finding: the relay's --latency-ms emulation added about 25 ms on Windows (socket timeouts round to the 15.6 ms timer):
  --latency-ms 100 measured 107..141 ms, p50 125. The 1 Nov live numbers therefore ran at about 125 ms (the model gives 12.4 and
  24.8 steps/s for k=1 and k=3 at 125 ms with a 36.6 ms frame, against 11.3 and 22.8 measured). Fixed here: with an emulated link
  the relay polls and sleeps in high-resolution steps; --latency-ms 100 now measures 100.8..111, p50 102.

1. MEASUREMENT
  NGPING|5|<token>|<slot>|<nonce> from the relay; the client answers NGPONG|5|<token>|<slot>|<nonce>|<held_us> when it reads it.
  The gate reads its socket only inside gate_tick: at once while it waits for a permit (blocking receive), otherwise in the drain
  after the frame. held_us is the time since the client last found its socket empty, 0 for a ping that arrived during a blocking
  receive; the round trip is in [measured-held, measured]. Pings every 20 ms while the relay holds tick 0 (every client is then
  blocked waiting: exact samples), every 200 ms during the session (one datagram each way per client, 46+60 bytes).
  The relay keeps both ends per sample: the plan and the report use the middle; session raises use the low end (a ping that
  waited for a frame does not raise k), lowering uses the high end, which leaves about a frame of hysteresis. Without the held
  field a 40 ms link read 55..72 ms p90 in the model (frames of 33 ms) and k flapped 2->3->2.

2. THE PLAN AT TICK 0
  Relay native_gate_relay.py --adaptive --lookahead KMAX [--adaptive-min, --adaptive-target-hz, --adaptive-margin-ms,
  --adaptive-samples, --adaptive-measure-seconds, --adaptive-headroom]; client native_gate_lookahead = 'adaptive',
  native_gate_lookahead_max = KMAX (default 4).
  Choice (tools/dev/native_gate_adaptive.py wanted_k): r = slowest client's p90, f = 1000/tick_hz, target = 1000/target_hz:
    k = clamp(ceil((r + margin + f)/target - 1), kmin, kmax); target = f gives k = ceil((r + margin)/f).
  This follows the model period = max(f, (r+f)/(k+1)): k frames must cover the round trip. Margin 10 ms by default. Pilot delay
  k+2+headroom, lag L = k+1. Tick 0 is held until 8 samples per client (--adaptive-samples) or 5 s after the last tick 0 report;
  a client with fewer than 2 samples stops the session (adaptive_unmeasured_slot_S). The hold costs about 0.2 s (8 pings 20 ms
  apart plus a round trip; 155..185 ms in the model); 40 samples took 0.9 s through the real relay on this PC.
  Announcement: entry 1 (start 0, lag, pilot) rides every permit (NGGO|5 ... |<epoch>|<start>:<lag>:<pilot>|...); tick 0
  releases permits 1..L, of which 1..L-1 carry no inputs. The client refuses a plan above native_gate_lookahead_max (190).
  Adoption (lockstep_adaptive.h adapt_pilot_adopt): the plan arrives with tick 0's permits, while stats.issued is 0. With wire
  pilot records armed the plan's delay replaces the armed one if pilot_config_valid and pilot_delay_covers accept it and no pilot
  prediction is armed (whose horizon is the armed delay); else 194. Every later reader of pilot_delay (staging, apply, stock
  first apply at capture start >= 3, UI replay, native hit claims) reads it at run time, so all of them follow from tick 1. The
  probe arms the pilot records with kmax+2 at the menu and, after a UI reload, with gate.adaptive_plan(). Player orders keep
  their configured delay, which the probe requires to be >= kmax+1 (their delay is a Lua local of native_gate_player_orders.lua,
  read once; making it follow the plan is a two-line change there, not made: not my file).
  Agreement: every client gets the identical permit text, and each permit names its source tick; the client checks it against
  the schedule it holds (191), the relay checks every report's epoch (adaptive_epoch / adaptive_unannounced) and that quiet
  ticks carry nothing. The digest comparison stays as it was.

3. CHANGES DURING A SESSION
  The schedule (Schedule in native_gate_adaptive.py, adapt_* in lockstep_adaptive.h, same rules and the same test numbers):
  entries (start, lag, pilot); a proposal sealed with tick c applies at c + lag(c).
  - Raise from start c (lag a -> b): permits c-1+a+1 .. c+b-1 carry no inputs (gaps). The window grows at once: report c-1
    releases them together with c-1+a.
  - Lower from start c (a -> b, d = a-b): ticks c-d..c-1 are quiet. Every client seals the empty proposal there (the probe does
    not call its adapters, so orders, hit claims and octree reports simply go one tick later); no permit carries them; report
    c-d-1 releases up to c+b-1 = c-d-1+a, so the window shrinks by d.
  - Over the other ticks the apply tick rises by exactly 1: nothing lost, duplicated or applied before it was sealed.
  - "Ahead by at least the old K+delay": an entry is announced only when its first changed tick (start, or its first quiet
    tick) is above the highest permit issued, so no client has sealed it (a client runs at most to the highest permit), and
    every later permit carries it. One entry in flight: the next only after every client's reports show the previous one.
  - Release rule in general: report t (not quiet) releases every permit up to c'+L(c')-1, c' the next non-quiet tick after t.
  - Policy (opt-in, every 2 s on the last interval's pings): raise to the k the low ends need; lower (--adaptive-decrease) to
    what the high ends allow after 3 intervals in a row. Never above pilot-2.
  Pilot records today: lockstep_pilot_inputs.h, lockstep_pilot_stock.h, lockstep_pilot_native.h (first apply),
  lockstep_ui_replay.h and lockstep_hit_native.h use one pilot_delay for the whole session. So with wire pilot records a session
  change may only raise k up to pilot-2 (the plan's --adaptive-headroom) and never lower it: a quiet tick would leave a record
  missing at capture+delay. The client refuses that (194) when the entry arrives, instead of stopping later with 91.
  What the pilot modules need for full changes (follow-up, not mine): delay_at(capture) = lag(capture)+1 from the schedule;
  staging uses it; at an apply tick without a record after a raise, hold the previous record (stock: no events, which holds the
  keys), not missing_record; at a quiet tick no capture, and the stock path carries the queued events into the next capture
  (pstk_build already queues); prediction horizon per tick. The model checks exactly this variant ('schedule' pilot records,
  apply = capture+lag+1): never late, never two for one tick.

4. STALLS AND DROPOUTS
  --stall-report-ms (default 1000): when the others have waited that long for a client's report of the next tick, the relay logs
  NATIVE_GATE STALL slot=S tick=T waited_ms=..., once per client and tick, and lists it in the report (adaptive.stalls). Waiting
  starts when the first report of that tick arrived (or the tick before was released, if later).
  --stall-drop-ms (default 0 = never): past it the relay drops S from that tick T: S's blob is empty in every later permit and the
  permit's mask names S (identical for everyone, monotone; the clients check both), its digest is no longer compared, it is
  answered NGDROP and sent nothing else (a client that sees itself dropped stops with 193). The others go on: in the model a
  client dying at tick 150 of 400 is reported after 0.5 s, dropped after 2 s, and the two others finish all 400 ticks with
  identical inputs. Without the drop bound everyone waits and the clients stop at their wait timeout (7), as today.
  What the drop does not solve (needs roadmap D15 drop/rejoin): the dropped slot's ship is still in every simulation. Its pilot
  records stop, so pilot_frame_records stops the session with 91 at the first missing record (fail closed, earlier and named,
  instead of a 60 s timeout). A dropped slot needs an agreed state for its ship (parked, autopilot, or removed through MD at an
  agreed tick), its player entity and owned assets, and rejoin needs the save/fork path of roadmap D14. Sessions without
  slot-bound inputs (orders, octree reports, census) already go on.

5. WIRE EXTENSION (for the peer-link, NAT and encryption work)
  Protocol 5 is a separate version number in every message; protocols 1-4 are unchanged and an adaptive relay refuses them
  (lookahead_mismatch_slot_S), an older relay drops protocol 5 (the client then times out with 7), an older DLL has no
  gate.lookahead_adaptive and the probe refuses. Full text: docs/LOCKSTEP-NSLOT-WIRE.txt, PROTOCOL 5.
    NGSTATE|5|<token>|<slot>|<tick>|<game>|<real>|<digest>|<clients>|<epoch>|<blob>       client -> relay
    NGGO|5|<token>|<slot>|<T>|<source or ->|<epoch>|<start>:<lag>:<pilot>|<mask>|<blobs>  relay -> client
    NGPING|5|<token>|<slot>|<nonce>  /  NGPONG|5|<token>|<slot>|<nonce>|<held_us>
    NGSTOP|5|<token>|<slot>|<tick>  /  NGDROP|5|<token>|<slot>|<tick>
  Notes for joining: (a) a permit is a function of (T, source, epoch, mask) and the sealed blobs; peer links that deliver blobs
  directly can keep the relay's permit as the referee if they carry source and epoch too. (b) The schedule must stay one per
  session, issued by one party (the relay), because quiet ticks constrain what every client seals. (c) Round trips are measured
  client-relay; with peer links the relevant quantity becomes the slowest path a blob takes, and the plan's r should be that.
  (d) Pings are cheap, unauthenticated beyond the shared token like everything else; an AEAD over the relay path covers them.
  (e) Stop reasons 190-194 are taken (free at this base, whose highest was 181).

10. LIMITS AND OPEN

  - Pilot records, the stock path, UI replay and hit claims keep one pilot delay per session (section 3): full session changes

  - Drop/rejoin of a player's ship and assets (D15): a dropped slot's ship goes neutral under protocols 4 and 5 (section 13);
    rejoining it is open.

14. DROP AND REJOIN IN JOIN SESSIONS (coordinator, 4 November 2026; roadmap 15, second part)
  Design: in a hot join session (relay --join; protocols 3 and 4) a drop rides the JOIN records instead of the permit mask.
  A rejoinable slot (relay --rejoin K, clients native_gate_join.rejoin={K}; never slot 1, never an absent slot) plays from
  the start; the pilot modules hold it in the absent mask with its rows its own from tick 0 (gate.pilot_absent(mask, rejoin):
  from 0, enrolled 0, seated at take-1 like every slot). The JoinBarrier's stall timer (--stall-report-ms, --stall-drop-ms)
  reports a present slot the others wait for and, past the drop bound, drops it if it may be (a --rejoin slot, or a slot
  absent at the start after its handover): from its first missing row c the relay stands in for it, and row c carries
  ABORT(K, reason 16 'dropped'), so every client applies gate.pilot_abort at the same tick and the slot is neutral from row c
  (the seat stays). The slot is then absent again: a later NGJOIN takes it through the normal hot join (SAVE_AT, the
  savers' package, catch-up, HANDOVER(K, H)); pilot_handover_apply already accepts a handover after an ABORT. The relay
  forgets the dropped client's address (its later reports are answered NGDROP|4, never counted), resets the slot's real
  clock base to the reference slot's (a rejoiner restores the saver's clocks; --relative-real compares elapsed time), and
  fails a join whose unsaved saver or whose joiner is dropped. The stock path's held-key release fires at the first silent
  row after an ABORT as after a mask drop; a rejoiner presents its ship from its new H+3, not during its catch-up. The
  driver and overlays accept --rejoin (the handoff names absent and rejoin slots); --join-observe (diagnostic, default off)
  counts a joiner's digest mismatches before H instead of stopping it, every line of such a run says diagnostic=join_observe.
