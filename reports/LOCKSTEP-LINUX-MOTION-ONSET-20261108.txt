Bounded diagnostic additions
----------------------------
The default-off per-tick Nav watch uses the existing canonical motion/timing
serializer for at most four saved identities over an explicit bounded window.
It does not advance or rewrite census state. Its independent evidence parser
requires the complete ordered tick/identity set, validates canonical metadata,
cross-checks matching census preimages and hashes, and rejects failed cleanup.
Mandatory watch receipts bypass optional status thinning.

A separate default-off path-transform observer can compare the native cached
transform with a live-transform candidate inside ordered path jobs. It returns
the native result unchanged, but the additional engine getter may have side
effects. It is a diagnostic experiment, not a passive or production-safe fix.
Its native behaviour has not yet been validated. It excludes the overlapping
step-chain hook and fails closed on ownership, bounds or buffer failures.

Longer nav-key control and trader-script divergence
--------------------------------------------------
The reversed-launch-order 24000-tick control on clean source 6b3dcb5 and the
same b53e4a7 binary fails. Its first full-world mismatch is again at 11520;
the per-tick watch first differs at 11515 in the motion of 8b613. The three
preceding watched ticks agree. At the first difference one peer has zero
linear velocity while the other has a small nonzero velocity. Watch and
detail bytes at 11520 reproduce their respective differing census leaves.
The earlier 12000-tick nav-key pass therefore does not establish a fix.

This control does not finish its requested duration. Both peers abort at
14436 with WORLD_MOTION_ERROR stage=nav_owner_invalid. That receipt has no
target identity, so it cannot identify which watch failed or prove that a
ship died. There are 121 complete census captures through 14400 and 25744
watch rows per peer through 14435, not the requested full-run coverage.
Both report ABORT active=0 restored=1; the controller finds no owned process
remaining and the same Steam process. Normal completion footers are absent.
The overall result remains FAIL, independently of the useful earlier data.

The retained script window 11360..11900 is internally complete: 69577 rows
per peer, contiguous absolute indices 1517751..1587327, exact boundary
cursors, and all 541 closed per-tick counts/keyset/drawset tallies match
their exported rows. Every entered scope in that window has a row. This
bounded evidence does not turn the aborted run into a passing test.

The first matched keyed scope with different draw counts is at tick 11396:
trade.find.free, saved owner 8b61b, outer Exec scope, PC 0x172..0x172. Its key,
initial RNG word and caller word agree. One peer consumes 56 generator steps,
the other 57; independent stepping reproduces both logged exit words. The
snapshot explicitly assigns 8b61b to ship 8b613's aipilot post. At 11424 the
scope membership/keyset differs; that missing call is not a matched PC-value
difference. No matched-scope PC difference appears in the retained window.

This ties an earlier script divergence to the pilot of the later diverging
ship. It does not yet identify a differing trade-offer list or prove the
origin of its inputs. Linux currently sorts due events but runs their native
handlers on the caller's RNG word. Windows battle-snapshot defaults enable
an independent RNG scope per delivery; its existing investigation describes
related trader/offer failures. An opt-in Linux scope is the next controlled
comparison. Native event type RVAs differ between platforms, so a Linux-only
scope does not by itself establish Windows/Linux seed parity.
