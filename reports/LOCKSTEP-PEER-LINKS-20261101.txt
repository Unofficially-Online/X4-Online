1. RESULT AND RECOMMENDATION
Recommendation: GO for a measured live test and for keeping the module (opt-in, default off); NO-GO for
making it a default or a public-session feature before the NAT and crypto modules are joined and the
live numbers in section 9 confirm the model.
- What it saves is input delay, not step rate. With adaptive delay (item 3) the relay-only session also
  reaches 30 steps/s, by raising k. Peer links reach 30 steps/s at a smaller k, because inputs apply at
  t+1+k and k now only has to cover the players' own distance; the relay's round trip is covered by m,
  which costs no input delay.
- Two players 5 ms apart with the relay 120 ms round trip away (G1): relay only needs k=4 (order delay
  167 ms, pilot 200 ms); peer links k=1 m=3 (order delay 67 ms, pilot 100 ms). At the same k=1 relay only
  runs 13.0 steps/s, peer links 30.0. One country, relay abroad (G2): 133/167 ms against 67/100 ms.
- No gain when the relay is on the way between the players or on a better route (G3, G4): the step then
  waits for the relay copy anyway; peer links cost nothing there either (same rate, same delay).
- One player pair that cannot connect (G8) puts every client back on relay speed: lockstep waits for the
  slowest record. Peer links only pay off when every pair connects (or a later 2-hop forward exists).
- Under 2 % datagram loss neither variant holds 30 steps/s (resends are 100 ms); at k=1 peer links still
  double the rate (24.4 against 12.2 steps/s); relay only needs k=4..8 for 26 steps/s.
- The permit is indeed unnecessary for most steps: in G1/G2/G7/G9 98-99 % of the steps started on peer
  records before their permit arrived; the permit then only confirms them (and stops the session if a
  copy differed). It cannot be dropped altogether: it is what bounds a cheat, a forged record, a dead
  relay or a desync to m ticks, and what ends the session.

2. WHAT LOCKSTEP ACTUALLY WAITS FOR (THE MODEL)
Protocol 4 (relay only, window k): the permit for tick T needs every client's report of T-1-k and comes
back through the relay. For client j: start_j(T) >= max_i(E_i(T-1-k) + up_i) + down_j, which includes
i = j: every client's own report makes the relay round trip r_j, even a client next to the relay whose
partner is far away (G5). Steady state: P = max(f, (r+f)/(k+1)) (reproduced exactly by the existing
SimulatedSession and by the new simulator, which matches it tick for tick).
Peer links (window k, peer window m): a client needs only the other players' records, start_j(T) >=
max_{i!=j}(E_i(T-1-k) + p_ij), plus T <= F + m, where F follows the relay permits. The cycles now run
through pairs (one-way latency p) and through the relay with m more ticks of slack:
  P = max(f, (p+f)/(k+1), (r+f)/(k+m+1))
The simulator asserts this within 3 % for r = 90..200 ms, p = 10..30 ms, k = 1..2, m = 0..7.
Consequences: the step still waits for every player's record (that is lockstep); what moves is which
path the record takes. Peer links help exactly when p is well below r, i.e. when the relay is far from
the players relative to their distance to each other. They also remove the "own round trip" term, which
is why even the relay-at-player-1 case (G5) gains (17.7 -> 27.3 steps/s at k=1).

4. DESIGN (src/native/engine/lockstep_peer.h; reference tools/dev/native_gate_peer.py)
- Rides on protocol 4 unchanged: NGSTATE|4 reports and NGGO|4 permits are byte-identical; the relay
  checks every tick (digests, clocks, timestep) exactly as before. A record sealed with t applies at
  t+1+k on every client, whether it came by peer or by permit: no new input delay rules.
- A client runs tick T only if T <= F + m (F = highest tick whose permit and all earlier ones arrived),
  on the permit or, without it, on all N peer records of T-1-k, never before the relay admitted tick 0
  for everyone (F >= 1) and never past the relay's last tick (named in NGPADDR). k + m <= 8 keeps every
  unconfirmed report inside the existing report ring, so resends stay possible.
- Fail closed: every permit is compared with the peer copies of its records, before or after its tick
  ran (104); two different peer copies of one record (105); another copy of a final permit (103). The
  client tells the relay (NGPFAIL), which fails the session and stops everyone at once (the simulator
  shows the whole equivocation case over in well under a second instead of the 60 s timeout). Every
  peer-run tick is confirmed by its permit within m ticks; at the session end a lost permit may be
  overtaken by the relay's NGSTOP, but those ticks' inputs are in the digests the relay compared (the
  INPUTS_1 receipts). Malformed, unauthenticated or unexpected peer datagrams are dropped, never fatal.
- Off by default; with gate.peer_links never called, every hook is a no-op and protocol 4 behaves as
  before (the existing lookahead C tests, the protocol 4 Linux engine scenarios and the relay suites
  still pass; a peer-off frame sends exactly one datagram and opens no socket, asserted).
- The relay stays the referee for timeouts and dropouts: a missing client stops everyone exactly as in
  protocol 4 (no client runs past 19+1+k when one stops reporting after tick 19); a digest mismatch at
  tick 40 stops everyone within 40+k+m ticks. Dropout handling (item 3/15) must later decide a drop at a
  tick no client can already have run on peer records, i.e. at least m ticks after the dropped slot's
  last report reached any client, and must take that slot's records in between from the clients (each
  holds them); until then a stalled player stops the session as today.
- Hooks: lockstep_lookahead.h (include, reset, resend start, permit check incl. stale permits, the take
  rule, wake on either socket while waiting, send after sealing: ~20 lines), both gates (forward
  declaration, close with the relay socket, start refuses an invalid window with 10, two Lua methods),
  the probe (native_gate_peer_window), the relay (--peer-window, NGPHELLO/NGPFAIL handling, report
  window +m). Specification: docs/LOCKSTEP-NSLOT-WIRE.txt, PEER LINKS.
- Relay extension, versioned: peer extension 1 (message family NGP*, version field 1). NGPHELLO (opt-in,
  from the peer socket), NGPADDR (all addresses + m + last tick, only once all N opted in), NGPREFUSE
  (off/mismatch: the client stays relay-only), NGPREC (peer to peer), NGPFAIL. An older relay drops
  NGPHELLO and the session runs relay-only; an older DLL makes the probe refuse (REFUSED peer_window).
- Bandwidth: each client sends N-1 extra datagrams per tick, about 160 bytes with a 28-byte pilot record
  (4 players at 30 Hz: 14 KB/s each way per client), at most about 600 bytes with a full 240-byte blob
  (8 players: 125 KB/s, 1 Mbit/s).

5. INTERFACES EXPECTED FROM THE PARALLEL MODULES (stubs in lockstep_peer.h and native_gate_peer.py)
NAT traversal (X4-Multiplayer-nat). One UDP socket per client for all peers (hole punching and UPnP
both work on one socket). C: point peer_nat at a peer_nat_api; Python: an object with these methods.
  unsigned local_port(void)
      port to bind the peer socket to (0: any); called once before bind, after a UPnP mapping.
  int route(SOCKET sock, unsigned slot, const struct sockaddr_in *advertised, struct sockaddr_in *route)
      after NGPADDR, every frame until it decides: 1 = send slot's records to *route from sock;
      0 = unreachable ("use the relay": that slot's records then come only with permits);
      -1 = still punching. advertised = the address the relay saw for that player's peer socket
      (its NAT mapping, i.e. the server-reflexive address hole punching needs). May send its own
      probes from sock.  Python: route(slot, (ip, port)) -> (ip, port) | None | 'pending'.
  int datagram(SOCKET sock, const struct sockaddr_in *from, const char *data, size_t length)
      every datagram on the peer socket that is neither the relay's answer nor a record from a routed
      peer (the module's own probes and punches): 1 = consumed.
  If the NAT module prefers one connected socket per pair, it can keep this contract by routing each
  pair to an address it relays locally, or the coordinator extends route() to return a socket.
Encryption and signing (X4-Multiplayer-crypto). Peer records only; the relay path is unchanged here.
C: point peer_crypto at a peer_crypto_api; Python: an object with these methods.
  size_t seal(const char *plain, size_t length, char *out, size_t capacity)
      AEAD (e.g. ChaCha20-Poly1305) of the whole datagram under the session key the relay hands out;
      the module owns nonces and framing; 0 = do not send.
  size_t open(const char *sealed, size_t length, char *out, size_t capacity)
      plain length, 0 = not sealed with the session key -> dropped, never fatal.
  int sign(const char *record, size_t length, char *signature, size_t capacity)
      this player's signature (e.g. Ed25519) over the record text before the signature field, lowercase
      hex, at most 256 characters, zero-terminated; 0 = do not send.
  int verify(unsigned slot, const char *record, size_t length, const char *signature, size_t sig_length)
      1 = genuine signature of slot's key (public half from the relay at session start).
Stub behaviour: no secrecy and no authenticity (seal/open copy, empty signature). A forged or spoofed
record can then stop a session (104), but never be applied unnoticed; sender addresses are pinned to
the routes the relay handed out. With the real modules an outsider cannot inject at all and a player
cannot send records as another; a player who sends different records to the relay and to its peers is
still caught by the permit check (signatures then also prove who did it).

6. PRIVACY
Players learn each other's public IP addresses and peer ports; that is inherent to direct links. So:
off by default; opt-in per PC (native_gate_peer_window) and per session (relay --peer-window); and
all-or-nothing: the relay hands out no address unless every client sent NGPHELLO. The relay already
knows every address; it never writes them to its report, status file or console (asserted). Players
should be told before enabling it (UI text is the integrator's). With the stub crypto, records travel
in clear text, as the relay path does today.

7. COMBINING WITH ADAPTIVE DELAY (item 3, X4-Multiplayer-adelay)
The two compose: measure the relay round trip r and, with peer links, the peer round trip per pair at
session start; choose k from the peer cycle (k >= p/f, at least 1) and m from the relay (k+m >= r/f,
k+m <= 8). The pilot/order delay checks are unchanged (pilot >= k+2, orders >= k+1). If any pair is
unreachable, choose k from the relay as today and m = 0 (records only, or off). A later change of k
during a session moves both windows at the agreed tick.
