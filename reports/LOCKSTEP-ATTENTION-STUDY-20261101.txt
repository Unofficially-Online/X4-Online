LOCKSTEP ATTENTION STUDY (roadmap C9: asymmetric attention) - 1 NOVEMBER 2026

Branch contrib/ferrite-attention (from smoke e457b4c9). Offline work only: the pinned X4 9.00
build (SHA-256 19750a65...6891) read with the private RE workspace (Ghidra project copy, capstone
sweeps over .pdata ranges), the shipped AI/MD scripts read for behaviour, the gate sources and the
census/tick-rate reports for measured numbers. No X4 was launched, no lane tool was run.
Tags: [F] read in the binary (address given, RVA) or in the shipped scripts; [M] measured earlier
and reported elsewhere (source named); [I] inference or estimate. Findings are described in our own
words; no decompiler output or game data is reproduced.

1. VERDICT
----------
Asymmetric attention in the sense of the roadmap item - each PC pays full price only near its own
player while all universes stay identical - is not feasible with lockstep on this engine. High and
low attention are two different simulations of the same object, not a cheap and an expensive
rendering of one simulation: another flight model, physics bodies or none, physical shots or
scripted damage per second, other AI script branches and other script timing, and the change of
level itself writes state (a ship is moved to a free spot, a station draws random numbers and builds
a region, scripts restart at a label, events reach MD). Every client therefore has to compute every
player's high-attention bubble, which is what the gate does today. Splitting the work (one client
computes a bubble and ships the result) is a partial return to state replication with an authority
boundary the engine does not have; bandwidth would be acceptable (about 0.6-4 Mbit/s per remote
sector [I]), the coupling and handover are not. What can pay off is making the shared work cheaper
for everyone (section 6c) and, piecemeal, moving genuinely cosmetic high-attention work out of the
shared step (6a). First, though, the price of one extra bubble has never been measured: the census
runs mostly had all players in one sector. Section 8 is a measurement plan with two default-off
diagnostics built for it (native_gate_view_points_mask, native_gate_sample_profile).

2. WHAT ATTENTION IS (mechanics)
--------------------------------
[F] Level: an int at component +0x1ec, 1..11. The shipped script schema lists the levels in this
order: unknown, ingalaxy, incluster, sectorvisible, insector, adjacentzone, visible, inzone, nearby,
adjacentroom, inroom; the numeric thresholds below match that order (visible = 7, nearby = 9) [F/I].
[F] Computation, vtable slot 703 (+0x15f8), per class, viewer-dependent (lockstep_view_points.h has
the list): the player's zone is 8; any other zone whose box distance to the player is below the
zone's extent plus a 50 km literal is 7, otherwise 6 (Zone 0x843b20); the player's sector is 5
(Sector 0x78d6a0); an object within its per-macro nearby radius of the player or camera is 9
(Object 0x6d20b0), the ship carrying the player 9 (Ship 0x7d5110), the interior holding the player 10
(Positional 0x753350), rooms up to 11 (Room 0x76e520). Components without their own attention take
the nearest ancestor's (Component 0x3d5600).
[F] SetAttention 0x3d5660: level 1 means "recompute through slot 703" for components that keep their
own attention (+0xd1); it writes +0x1ec, sets bit 0x40 in three dirty words (+0x11c, +0x19c, +0x1e4),
calls slot 701 (+0x15e8) as OnAttentionChanged(old, wasHigh, new, isHigh), stores the new high flag
through slot 690, posts ChangedAttentionEvent (old, new; what MD sees as
event_object_changed_attention [I]) and, when crossing 5/6, ChangedAdjacentZoneAttentionEvent. It
returns early only when neither the level nor the high flag changed.
[F] "High" is slot 688 (+0x1580): Component 0x3e6a10 answers level >= 7 and the parent high too
(recursively, also on the published view copy of the parent); Sector 0x6a2390 answers level > 3;
Ship 0x7d49c0 adds a parent-class condition; Bullet 0xd4eef0 is never high before its start time.
Slot 689 answers the previous flag: computed like 688 for most classes, stored for Zone (0x5be8c0
reads +0x541), Ship (0x7c86e0) and Entity (0x4b76f0).
[F] Propagation and re-evaluation: Component slot 701 0x3d5880 pushes the new level into both child
lists (children with own attention recompute). The object attention timer 0x6d1ff0 (every
0.75 s + random 0..0.5 s, lockstep_attention.h) is called by Object::Move 0x6ccf90 only inside its
high branch; Zone::Move 0x834950 re-evaluates zones above 5; Container::Move 0x41a430 re-evaluates
its children above 8. Low objects are not re-evaluated by a timer of their own; their level changes
through their parents and explicit calls (ConnectTo, camera and cutscene events, the gate's refresh).
[F] 131 functions call slot 688, 236 functions read +0x1ec directly (capstone sweep over every .pdata
range of the image; the lists stay in the private workspace).

3. WHAT SWITCHES WITH ATTENTION
-------------------------------
3a. At 7 ('visible', the main switch)
- Flight model [F]. The wanted model comes from slot 1052: Ship 0x7d5ba0 / Object 0x6e1e80 answer 3
  (LinearFlightControlModel) when not high, 2 (highway) inside a highway, 5 (spline) or 6 (steering)
  otherwise, 4 for mass traffic. Object slot 701 0x6d2520 stores the high flag in the ship's
  NavigationController (+0xd8) and queues a SetFlightControlModelOrder (0xeac900 -> PathingManager
  AddOrder); the order builds the model in 0xe87cf0 (linear 0xeb1a10, steering 0xec40c0, spline
  0xeb4f40). The tour race of the census (battle drops to low attention, ships switch to the linear
  model at 2467) is this path [M, census].
- Physics bodies [F]. Destructible slot 701 0x51aa10 asks slot 876 whether the object should have a
  body (Positional 0x747630: high and timing; Ship 0x7d7f60: also a physics scene for its zone via
  slot 943 and within 250 km of the scene anchor, squared distance 6.25e10) and creates (slot 904
  0x51bb90) or destroys (slot 905 0x51bc80) the body. Natively a zone has a scene only in the player
  object's sector (scene A) or the cutscene camera's (B), so stock X4 runs physics in one sector
  (lockstep_view_scenes.h); the gate adds scenes for view points 2..4. Every Jolt job runs on the
  MoveWorker inside XPhys::Scene::Step 0xba600 (lockstep_physics_trace.h): physics is serial on the
  critical path, scene after scene.
- Weapons and damage [F]. The shoot_at script action (ShootAction 0xbd1e90) and AI::ShootAtAction
  0x10a8460 refuse below 'visible' with an error string to that effect; Weapon slot 1042 0x81ccb0
  answers 0 at level <= 6; the fire path (Weapon slot 998 0x8185b0) passes "level < 9" into a transform
  computation (0x3e2120) [purpose I]. In the shipped combat scripts (fight.attack.object.*) the
  low-attention branch computes an attack strength, removes ammunition and applies damage once per
  second over a randomly long attack run; the high branch flies and shoots with real projectiles.
- AI scripts [F]. AIDirector::Move copies the controlled object's level into the script instance
  (store at 0x106e364, +0x128) and selects the action list for it (0x1074850); the loader enforces
  that labels match across levels ("mismatched labels" message), so a script switches branch at a
  label. Of 178 base AI scripts 31 have a separate 'visible' branch, 2 a 'nearby' branch (mass
  traffic), 24 also test .attention in expressions; 33 of 221 MD files use attention (NPC
  instantiation, signal leaks, missions). High branches wait 100-500 ms between steps where low
  branches wait seconds (fighter attack: 1 s damage ticks, 1-7 s runs).
- Transitions that write state [F]. Ship slot 701 0x7d5270, when a ship becomes high (outside a
  highway, under further conditions not decoded), asks the zone for a free position (0x835b80) and
  moves the ship there (slot +0x1468).
  Station slot 701 0x7f8f10 on becoming high calls 0x7f9bc0 (draws Random) and 0x7fa5c0 (the
  'station_region_macro' region), and tears both down when it drops. Destructible 0x51aa10 switches
  hull-damage effect states on and off (0x522120/0x522460/0x5227a0, 0x522e30/0x522ec0/0x522f50).
  Defensible 0x4d4da0 calls slots +0x1db0/+0x1db8 and clears per-object lists when dropping [purpose I].
- Also consulting "high" [F, call sites only]: docking and internal storage (0x540530, 0x5423c0,
  0x417a40, 0x4184b0, 0x417ef0), towing (TowingMovementController), recycling (0x5783c0),
  countermeasures (0x4ecb90), job-ship spawning (JobEngine path 0xf03e40), region fields (Region::Move
  0x767410, Field slot 10 0xedc540), detectors, production/processing modules, gates, mines,
  lockboxes, signal leaks, missiles, engines (0x54bcc0 family).
3b. At 9 ('nearby') and above
- Interiors [F]: crossing 9 posts GeneratePropsEvent / removes props (Container 0x3f7770),
  InteriorsDespawningEvent (Controllable 0x464160, Defensible 0x4d4da0) and GenerateMazesEvent
  (Destructible 0x51aa10); walking bodies and crowds work at 9-10 (BodyClass::Move 0x380dd0,
  CrowdAgentController 0x5732d0, NPC::Move 0x6bc420 at 10).
- Physics detail [F]: crossing 9 replaces physics shapes (ReplacePhysicsShapesEvent or slots 878/879,
  Positional 0x753480); Ship::Move 0x7cc810 runs its body upkeep and detailed part only above 8.
- Random impulses [F]: Object::Move above 8 draws three Random::FloatState values and applies them
  through slot +0x1b88 when a configured magnitude is set.
- A registry add/remove at 9 in Ship slot 701 (0x1410b2820 / 0x1410b2d60) [F; purpose I].
3c. At 5/6 and 8 (zones and sectors) [F]: Zone::Move re-evaluates above 5 and above 7 runs the mass
traffic decision (0x83ef20) and temp-zone removal near the camera; Sector::Move 0x784230 tests 5;
Highway::Move 0x5f4a40 takes its spline parameter from the camera for levels 5..7 (UniverseFrameUpdate
from the player's controllable at >= 8, lockstep_region_camera.h); the hazard DamageField 0xee16a0
has a separate low-attention pass below 5 (census finding 15).

4. DO HIGH AND LOW ATTENTION PRODUCE THE SAME STATE?
----------------------------------------------------
No, and not only in rounding. For the same ship and the same orders [F for the switch, I for the
size of the difference]:
- motion: linear model (kinematic, straight segments) versus steering/spline model with forces
  through a Jolt body, collision response and avoidance - different positions within seconds;
- combat: per-projectile hits on physics shapes versus scripted damage per second from attack
  strength; different hull/shield values, kill times, ammunition and loot;
- AI: other branches with other waits, so decisions fall on other ticks and draw other random
  numbers; MD reacts to the change events themselves;
- transitions: relocation to a free spot, station regions with random draws, body creation (the
  census wreck at 2492 took its pose from the cache at body creation), interiors, props and their IDs.
Only the parts that never read attention are the same at both levels: the economy's production and
trade bookkeeping, the Gravidar scans, most MD logic [I]. A sector that is high on one client and low on
another diverges at the first tick. This is why the gate makes attention a function of the shared
view points (lockstep_view_points.h) and keeps every client-local trigger out (view_triggers,
view_ui, camera_context), and why lockstep cannot give a PC a "cheap copy" of someone else's sector.

5. WHY EVERY PC PAYS FOR EVERY BUBBLE TODAY
-------------------------------------------
The gate evaluates attention once per view point (every pilot slot's ship) on every client and takes
the maximum (native_gate_view_points 'players'), keys the attention timer for every object
(attention_all), gives view points 2..4 a physics scene (view_scenes), a stand-in camera for region
content, mass traffic, effects and highway parameters (region_camera group), and blocks per-client
re-evaluations. Result: with players in n sectors every PC simulates n bubbles at high attention,
n physics scenes stepped serially on its MoveWorker, n regions of asteroid content, n mass traffic
areas. Stock X4 never does this (one physics scene, one camera); the cost grows with n.
Per-step cost today [M, LOCKSTEP-TICK-RATE-20261030]: two instances on one PC, 60 Hz play stack,
frame 28.9-31.5 ms median; Pbare (near-minimal gate) 23.6 ms; sim_us (the MoveWorker's frame) about
24 ms; region group 2.65 ms, traffic+region join 2.7-3.3 ms, pathing pass 3.3 ms for ~9,740 agents,
reads up to 3.8 ms CPU, probe 0.9 ms. The MoveWorker also renders (VBI slot 10: every universe
camera, lockstep_render_rng.h), so sim_us is simulation plus UI plus render preparation. No run has
measured the price H of one extra bubble.

6. OPTIONS
----------
(a) Compute shared what affects state, make presentation-only high-attention work local.
    Already per client and outside the shared step: rendering, sounds (ship sounds start/stop on
    camera events), engine-glow render parameters (EngineGlowValueSource set in Object slot 701),
    HUD, cockpit, map. These are not where a remote bubble costs: a PC does not render a sector its
    camera is not in.
    Cosmetic in effect but implemented as shared simulation objects (component IDs, RNG, events):
    effect objects (weapon fire, impacts, hull damage states, effect fields), room props and interior
    mazes (>= 9), walking crowds (>= 9/10), mass traffic, asteroid field fill. Each could be spawned
    only on the client whose player is near, if it is taken out of the shared ID and RNG streams on
    every client (the runtime_ids local lists and the render RNG split are the pattern) and if no
    simulation code reads it - mass traffic and asteroids are hit, mined and scripted, so they are
    not candidates. Feasible piece by piece; gain bounded by those subsystems' share of H [I: small,
    likely well under 20% of H; the sampler's spawn_effect mark gives the first number].
    Physics, flight model, weapons and AI branches are state: not candidates.
(b) Split the work: each client simulates its own bubble and ships the result to the others.
    Bandwidth [I]: a moving object needs pose, velocities, hull/shield; quantised about 25-40 bytes,
    at 30 Hz 0.75-1.2 KB/s per object. A quiet sector with ~100 moving objects about 0.6-1 Mbit/s,
    a battle with ~400 (ships, drones, missiles) 2.5-4 Mbit/s plus damage/creation/destruction
    events, per remote sector per receiving client; four players in four sectors: 2-12 Mbit/s down
    per client, the relay fans out N x (N-1) streams. Acceptable on broadband, not the blocker.
    Blockers [I, from the code paths above]: (1) no authority boundary - any AI script, MD cue, trade
    or Gravidar scan anywhere reads objects of any sector synchronously, so the receivers must hold
    the remote bubble's full state at the same tick the owner computed it, while the owner's bubble
    reads the shared universe of that same tick: the cycle closes inside one step and a delay breaks
    identity; (2) the remote copies must not simulate (their Move, AI and timers suppressed for those
    objects only) yet stay readable, a new engine mode for every object class; (3) handover when a
    player leaves, joins or two bubbles meet needs the bubble's complete state including AI/MD script
    state, effectively a partial save/load at a step; (4) attention changes inside the bubble (objects
    entering and leaving it) are themselves state transitions to replicate. This is the state
    replication architecture the project left for lockstep, plus lockstep. Not recommended.
(c) Lower the cost of the shared high-attention work (symmetric on every client, so identity holds).
    - Physics: scenes are independent physics systems in different sectors, yet stepped one after the
      other on the MoveWorker. Stepping scenes C/D on worker threads in parallel with A/B, with contact
      callbacks (RecordContact) buffered per scene and applied in scene order after the join, keeps
      determinism by construction [I; research item, needs the callbacks' reads audited].
    - Bubble size: the 'visible' margin is a 50 km literal in Zone slot 703 and the body radius
      250 km in Ship slot 876. A symmetric knob for a smaller margin (applied for every view point on
      every client) cuts the objects at high attention [I]; gameplay cost: farther ships fly the linear
      model and fight by script. Decide with the level histogram (VIEW_POINTS levels=) and play.
    - The work already built in the tick-rate study: native_gate_tick_hz 30, path_budget, pass_stride,
      fast_reads [M: 30 Hz + budget 100 + stride 2 + fast reads = 33.3 ms per tick, 1.00x, 5.8-6.8 ms
      spare]. These shrink U, the universe-wide part, which every PC pays once however many bubbles.
    - One X4 per PC: every number above is two instances sharing one CPU and GPU; players run one.
      Expect a sizeable reduction per instance [I]; measure (8.4).
(d) A dedicated server that takes the heavy sectors.
    A server that is one more lockstep peer (roadmap D13) keeps the world alive but takes no work off
    the clients: lockstep needs every peer to compute everything. A server authoritative for heavy
    sectors that streams them is (b) with one authority - same blockers. A server that simulates
    everything while clients only present streamed state leaves lockstep altogether (the earlier
    replica architecture, whose NPC scene sync proved unreliable). None is a cheap win.

7. RECOMMENDATION
-----------------
1. Keep symmetric attention: every PC simulates every player's bubble; do not start a hybrid.
2. Measure H, the price of one extra bubble, and U, the price of the universe without any bubble,
   with the plan in section 8 (two default-off diagnostics on this branch). The upper bound of what
   any asymmetric scheme could save per PC with players in n sectors is (n-1) x H.
3. Decide by the numbers [I, thresholds]:
   - H below ~15% of the step for a normal sector: close C9; spend the effort on U (C10, 6c).
   - H large and the profile shows physics_step/jolt_update dominating: parallel scene stepping (6c).
   - H large and dominated by the Move tree / AI branches: the symmetric bubble-size knob (6c),
     judged in play.
   - effects/props/crowds a large share: localise them one by one (6a).
Expectation before measuring [I, low confidence]: a quiet bubble costs a few ms of the ~24 ms
MoveWorker frame, a battle bubble 10 ms or more (projectiles, steering, physics, short AI waits);
U dominates in quiet play, H in battles.

8. MEASUREMENT PLAN (for the coordinator; live runs)
----------------------------------------------------
Setup for every run: the current play stack (Play41P-type overlay, own cockpits, stock demo pilots,
no captures: world_hash stride off or large), native_gate_tick_hz = 30, native_gate_frame_timing,
native_gate_mass_traffic 'sync' (it closes the sim window, so sim_us and the profile's step= are
meaningful), physics_scenes 'viewpoints' + view_scenes, 1803 ticks, each run twice (noise ~2 ms).
Read with census_perf --native (frame), the FRAME_TIMING sim_us median, VIEW_POINTS levels= and the
PHYSICS trace body counts where on, and census_profile.py for the sampler.
8.1 Zero-cost first look: census_perf --native on existing logs of scene-n1/n2/n3 (3-4 clients in
    3-4 sectors) against p4-C / p4-causeb2 (4 clients, one sector) - confounded by fixture and config,
    only an order of magnitude.
8.2 The price of a bubble (same world, same placement, only attention differs):
    M1 two players in two sectors: slot 2's ship moved to a second, busy sector at tick 30 (the cam-C1
       placement) - n = 2.
    M2 = M1 + native_gate_view_points_mask = 1 (slot 2 is no view point on any client: its sector at
       low attention everywhere, no scene B) - n = 1, slot 2 elsewhere.
    M3 = M1 + mask = 0 (no view point at all) - n = 0: U plus the gate. Scripted pilots only.
    M0 both players in one sector (baseline of today's census runs).
    H2 = sim(M1) - sim(M2); H1 = sim(M2) - sim(M3); U ~ sim(M3). All runs must pass the relay with
    0 mismatches (the mask is identical on every client, so identity must hold; a failure here is a
    finding in itself). Repeat M1/M2 with slot 2 in a quiet sector and in the war fixture's battle.
8.3 Where the time goes: M1 and M2 with native_gate_sample_profile = 2 (2 ms period, ~500 samples
    per second on the MoveWorker; expect pause_mean_us 20-40, i.e. ~1-2% slowdown - compare one
    M1 with and without the sampler to confirm). census_profile.py M1 --against M2 gives the per-mark
    difference in ms per step: physics_step/jolt_update, ai_director, md_scheduler, zone_move,
    ship_move/object_move, weapon_fire, spawn_effect, region_move, agent_pathing, set_attention,
    render_cameras, ui_move, job_engine. Check walked=samples and faults=0 in SAMPLE_PROFILE_STOP.
    For names of the inner rows pass the private workspace's symbols CSV with --names (not in git).
8.4 One instance per PC: M1 with the two clients on two PCs (or one client on the laptop) against M1
    on one PC: sim_us per instance without CPU contention.
8.5 Bubble size (only if 8.2 makes it worth it): count objects per level from VIEW_POINTS levels= and
    a capture; a knob for the 50 km margin would be a new default-off diagnostic, not built.

9. DIAGNOSTICS ADDED (default off, Windows gate only)
-----------------------------------------------------
native_gate_view_points_mask = 0..15 (lockstep_view_points.h, gate.view_points_mask): bit k-1 = pilot
  slot k counts as a view point (default all). Masked slots leave the latched list on every client,
  so every module that follows the list (scenes, stand-ins, policy) follows the reduced list; at the
  first latch the live player object's chain is recomputed. Needs native_gate_view_points 'players';
  before the first admitted frame. Status VIEW_POINTS ... mask= masked=. Measurement only: a masked
  player's ship flies the low-attention model and cannot fire.
native_gate_sample_profile = 1..50 ms (lockstep_sample_profile.h, gate.sample_profile/_status/_row):
  a sampler thread suspends the MoveWorker every period, walks its stack with RtlVirtualUnwind over
  function tables located at arm (X4, the gate DLL, every loaded module; stack pointers checked
  against the thread's TEB bounds, under SEH, no loader/heap/gate lock while suspended) and counts 21
  engine-function marks (inclusive), the innermost X4 function or call site, leaf classes and the
  pause. It follows ui_thread when snapshot boot adopts the in-game MoveWorker after arming
  (retargets=). Reads only. Status SAMPLE_PROFILE per tick, rows SAMPLE_PROFILE_ROW at stop.
tools/dev/census_profile.py: shares, binomial error, ms per step (step share x median sim_us), and
  --against for the A/B difference; optional --names for private symbol names.
check_gate_options.py knows both keys. Tests: test_lockstep_view_points.h (test_view_mask),
test_lockstep_sample_profile.h (tables and lookups against RtlLookupFunctionEntry, chained owners,
a live suspended worker thread walked top->mid->leaf, bounds, tally, rows, overflow, status widths,
arm/sample, following a new MoveWorker, restore; repeated 4 times under load, stable),
tests/test_native_gate_probe.py (arming, refusals, status and stop rows),
tests/test_native_gate_check_options.py, tests/test_census_profile.py. A one-thread sampler adds a
known risk: an exception during a walk is dispatched while the MoveWorker is suspended; if that
thread held the loader's function-table lock exclusively at that moment (a module load) the
process would hang. Walks validate every stack pointer first; faults= counts any that happen.

10. LIMITS
----------
- Static reading: the handlers named in section 3 were read for what they switch, not every callee;
  "purpose" notes marked [I] are not verified. The 131 slot-688 callers were classified by name and
  strings, the important ones read.
- The level names-to-numbers mapping rests on the schema order plus the thresholds (consistent at
  5, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11), not on a table read from the binary.
- No live number for H exists; every estimate of H in this report is [I] until 8.2 has run.
- The sampler is tested on this PC's own threads; on X4 it will see JIT-compiled Lua frames as
  unknown code (counted, the walk stops there) and frames of modules loaded after arm likewise.
- Linux gate: neither diagnostic is ported (no view points module there yet).
