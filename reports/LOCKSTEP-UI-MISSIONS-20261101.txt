Builds on reports/LOCKSTEP-UI-REPLAY-20261030.txt (the replay) and reports/LOCKSTEP-UI-GUARD-20261101.txt (the guard, P3).

1. DECISION: HOW MISSIONS WORK IN MULTIPLAYER
1a The user's decision (1 November, through the coordinator)
  Model (c), personal missions per player, in every session mode: each player has its own offers, missions, rewards and
  reputation. Story and plot missions (d) are per player too, as a separate work item with its own testing, handling and
  rewriting (section 5 lists what they need). Conversations follow (c). The base built now is (a): slot 1's missions and
  conversations replayed solidly on every client, slots 2+ refused until (c) exists.

1b The models that were compared (what each needs, how hard)
  (a) slot 1 only (today's state, made solid): accept/decline (SignalObject), abort, active mission, briefings and the
      conversation calls of slot 1 run on every client at the same step; slots 2+ refused with an info line. Needs nothing of
      MD (MD's player is slot 1's [F 2f]); needs the replay of five FFI exports and eight Lua globals and a reference for
      mission/offer IDs (MD cues, not components). Contained: BUILT here (section 3). Cost for people: player 2 has no
      missions; player 2 sees player 1's missions in its own lists (one mission manager per process) and player 1's
      conversation dialogs on its screen (one conversation manager per process).
  (b) coop shared missions (any player accepts, the group owns it, the shared wallet pays): mechanically small (replay slot
      k's accept canonically, as slot 1's), but objectives measured from the player (393 player.* reads in the 37 gm_*.xml,
      195 of them player.entity [F count]: sector checks, distances, inventory) stay slot 1's; a mission accepted by player 2
      near player 2 would be generated and judged around player 1. Rejected by the user's decision.
  (c) personal missions per player (CHOSEN): MD must run a mission's cue tree with "the player" = slot k's player, the
      mission manager and conversation manager must be slot k's, rewards and reputation slot k's faction's (factions mode)
      or a per player account (coop), offers generated around slot k. Needs C1..C6 of section 4; large; not contained in this
      step (section 4b says why and gives the order).
  (d) story/plot: player-specific state (MD globals, the player entity's blackboard), cutscenes on the player's view,
      teleports of the player, plot flags. Options were: disable in multiplayer, slot 1 only, or per player. User: per
      player, separately (section 5).

1c Order to build
  1. (a) for slot 1, then the people test M2 (section 7) to learn what a real conversation and briefing do on two clients.
  2. C1 MD slot scope (the foundation: player.* = slot k's inside slot k's cue trees), with its own read-only live probe.
  3. C2 per slot mission manager and conversation manager (swapped with the slot scope and client k's presentation).
  4. C5 replay of slot k's mission/conversation calls inside slot k's scope; C6 notifications to slot k's presentation.
  5. One generic mission type end to end for slot 2: GM_Find_Object (section 4c), then C3 (rewards, reputation) and C4
     (offers around slot k), then the other generic types one audit at a time (section 4d).
  6. Non-mission conversations (1d), then story/plot (section 5), one plot at a time.

1d Conversations that are not missions follow the same model
  Per player under (c): the comm/talk menus of station managers and pilots (conversations.xml 'default'), trade through a
  conversation (gTradeAction_start on player.computer [F conversations.xml]), hiring and crew delivery
  (SignalObjectWithNPCSeed*, npc_missions), on-foot NPC use cases (NPC_UseCases on player.entity [F replay report 14]),
  faction representatives and diplomacy agents (their relation changes are the slot's faction's in factions mode).
  World-level, not per player: none known; a conversation is always between "the player" and an actor [I: the 642
  listeners of event_conversation_started in the base game and the installed DLCs/mods were counted, not audited one by one].
  In (a) they all are slot 1's (replayed) or refused (slots 2+), like missions.

2. WHAT THE BINARY AND THE SCRIPTS SAY [F unless marked]
2a The mission manager is X4::GameClient's, one per process
  GameClient::Init 0x1223ce0 -> 0x12334b0 allocates 0x3d0 bytes, constructs with 0x1137710 and stores the pointer at
  0x6cf1658 (GameClient::Shutdown clears it); 131 functions reference that pointer. Offers +0x48..+0x50 (entries of 0x350
  bytes, ID at +0), missions +0x60..+0x68 (entries of 0x5d0 bytes, ID at +0). No owner or player field was found in the
  paths read (AbortMission, SetActiveMission, StartBriefing, StopBriefing, MissionBriefingInteractButtonClicked): missions
  belong to "the" player; there is no per-player key to use [F for these paths, I for the whole class].
2b Mission and offer IDs are MD cues
  Each export above finds its entry by ID, then U::ComponentRegistry::Find(g_UniverseIDMap 0x6cf1500, id, 0) and a
  dynamic cast from U::UniverseID to MD::Cue (type descriptors 0x31fc508 -> 0x31fd4f8; the UniverseID base is at offset 0
  of MD::Cue, vtable 0x2c75170). So the ID is the cue instance's runtime ID (the shared counter: the same on every client
  under native_gate_runtime_ids), not a component. The replay's component reference (Find(map, id, 4) and a portable
  identity, which world_identity_portable keeps for saved and created components) does not cover it [I], so an accept was
  "unmapped" (guard report 4b's open question); the cue reference of 3c answers it.
2c What each entry does to MD (event numbers from md_keywords.csv)
  AbortMission 0x175860                 Cue_RaiseCueEvent(cue, 0x63b event_mission_aborted) at once
  SetActiveMission 0x1b7010             0x113b760(manager, entry) / 0x113b920(manager,0,0,1): the active mission (guidance)
  MissionBriefingInteractButtonClicked  0x1b49c0 -> 0x640 event_briefing_interact_button_clicked (rml_buildstation,
                                        rml_deliver_fleet, rml_rescueship_2 listen)
  StartBriefing 0x1bbcb0                mission or offer; submission: 0x641; else 0x11228d0 -> 0x63e event_briefing_started,
                                        event.param = the render target texture name(s)
  StopBriefing 0x1bc9e0                 submission: 0x642; else 0x1122990 (event_briefing_cancelled [I: the 0x63f path])
  SignalObject 'accept'/'decline'       event_object_signalled on the offer's client; GenericMissions.AcceptMission does not
                                        read the third argument (the offer ID) [F genericmissions.xml 1058..]
2d A hole in the guard, closed: StartBriefing and StopBriefing were not inventoried
  The gm_* briefing cues (97 play_cutscene in 37 gm_*.xml [F count]) react to event_briefing_started by creating a
  presentation cluster and objects (e.g. GM_Find_Object.DisplayCutscene: create_presentation_cluster, create_object of a
  lockbox [F]): on the one client that opened the briefing, shared runtime IDs were taken. Both exports are now in the
  inventory's missions group (tools/dev/ui_guard_inventory.py; regenerated lockstep_ui_guard_table.h: 282 entries, the two
  new ones resolved and pinned like the rest).
2e The conversation manager is X4::GameClient's, one per process
  GameClient::Init stores it at 0x38ee230 (0x1224bd1); 71 functions reference it. It is updated in U::UniverseFrameUpdate
  (0x9fdef0 -> 0x1177d60) at game time; its state +0x1a0 is set to 1 at init and changed by 0x1178450 (fragment 0x11784a2)
  in that update, which posts U::StartDialogEvent (deferred, thread block time) when entering states 4/5 with options at
  +0x80 -> +0x60..+0x68. StartConversationFromMenu 0x276980 only queues a request (+0x1c0) while the state is 1 and
  0x11792e0 answers false. The dialog's UI flag 0x38f4bfb (DialogMenuShown/Hidden) is read by UI code only (0xaf12b0,
  CUIController vfunc66), not by the manager's update. [I] So the manager is driven by MD and replayed calls, not by a
  client's screen: equal on every client if the replayed calls are; the receipt checks it every tick (3d).
2f "The player" in MD is the singleton
  Scripts::EvaluateKeywordProperty 0xcd5910 (player.*, faction.*, ...) calls U::GetPlayerSlot(0x3d196f0) 26 times and reads
  0x3d196f0 73 times: player.entity, .ship, .sector, ... come from the Universe's player slot +0x230, the very slot the pilot
  scope swaps to P_k (lockstep_pilot_slots.h). reward_player (Scripts::RewardPlayerAction::Execute 0xbc75a0) pays through
  U::FactionClass::AddMoney(g_PlayerFaction 0x38f4bd0). Generic offers follow slot 1: GenericMissions.Manager.OnSectorChange
  listens on event_object_changed_sector object="player.entity", EvaluateSectorMissions generates in player.sector, offers
  are removed on the player's sector change (OfferMission RemoveOnSectorChange).
2g The active mission is read by MD and AI
  player.activemissionwaypoint (aiscripts/move.autopilot.xml: the player's autopilot following highways; conversations.xml
  'way to mission objective' choice), player.activemissiontype (genericmissions.xml PlotMissionInterrupted). With
  native_gate_guidance_local a client's own guidance is a mission there and the active one on that client alone (replay
  report 14): a conversation of slot 1 while client 2 has its own guidance may offer different choices on the two clients
  [I]. In (a) slot 1's SetActiveMission is replayed (the canonical active mission stays identical), slot 2's refused.

3. (a) BUILT: SLOT 1'S MISSIONS AND CONVERSATIONS ON EVERY CLIENT (default off, stop reason 164)
  native_gate_ui_replay lists 'missions' (bit 4096) and/or 'conversation' (bit 8192); true does not include them. Both need
  native_gate_ui_guard with their groups ('all' or a list with missions/conversation): the guard's hook is where the native
  capture happens and where slots 2+ are refused. Accepting an offer also needs 'events' (it is a SignalObject).
3a Missions (op 0x48, lockstep_ui_missions.h): the guard's hook (uiguard_decide) asks uimis_guard before refusing. On slot
  1's client, in a local phase, AbortMission, SetActiveMission, MissionBriefingInteractButtonClicked, StartBriefing and
  StopBriefing become an event: which, the mission/offer as a cue reference (3c), StartBriefing's texture (48 bytes at most)
  and flag, StopBriefing's flag; SetActiveMission(0) a zero reference. The menu gets what it expects (SetActiveMission: true;
  the others are void). X4's function does not run there. Every client applies at capture + delay in the probe's frame, in
  call order with the other Lua-run events (the replay's bypass: the guard passes), through the export with the resolved ID
  (gate.ui_missions_run). A cue gone on a client: nothing there (counted, the same everywhere when the worlds agree); a cue
  that is another one: stop (ui_ref_mismatch). Slots 2+, an ID that names no cue, a texture too long: the guard's refusal
  (UI_GUARD_REFUSED row, info line).
3b Conversations (op 0x49): the Lua core wraps StartConversationFromMenu, StartSubConversationFromMenu, ProceedFromMenu,
  ReturnFromMenu, CancelConversation, UnsuspendConversation, SelectDialogOption, SelectBackOption by name (Helper's
  closeMenuForNewConversation/Section/SubConversation/AndReturn/AndCancel reach them so). Slot 1: captured with the core's
  codec (actors as component references, convparam tables as tables), applied on every client through the stock global.
  Slots 2+: refused (UI_REPLAY_REFUSED why=slot) with an info line at most every 90 ticks ("Multiplayer: <name> works for
  player 1 only for now - nothing happened."). The four without component arguments are captured natively as well, in the
  guard's hook, when a script holds the C function itself (the core UI's dialog menu, ui/core/lua/dialogmenu.lua
  leaveDialogMenu -> SelectDialogOption(button); whether it sees the wrapped global is unknown [I]): the button read with
  lua_tonumber, the same event bytes as the Lua capture. The arming refuses when one of the eight globals is missing.
3c Cue references: the ID (8 bytes) and a 32-bit check of the ID and the cue's script module name (cue +0x160 definition,
  +0x150 module, +0x18 name, as lockstep_cue_name.h reads it; the vtable must be MD::Cue's). The Lua codec has two new tags
  (8 a cue as Lua ID, 9 as 64-bit ID), tried after the component reference: the events family's SignalObject(actor, 'accept',
  offer) now encodes when the missions or conversation family is armed (gate.ui_missions_cue_ref / _resolve). Without them
  a cue stays unmapped (refused, as before).

4. (c) PERSONAL MISSIONS PER PLAYER: WHAT IT NEEDS, WHY NOT IN THIS STEP, THE PATH
4a What "the mission belongs to slot k" needs
  C1 MD slot scope. MD frames of slot k's cue trees run with the singleton = P_k (pslot scope k), so every player.* of
     those cues is slot k's [F 2f: the evaluator reads the slot the scope swaps]. Pieces: (i) ownership: a root cue instance
     is slot k's when slot k's replayed call created it (accept, offer generation for k), descendants inherit (walk of the
     cue's parent chain, or a table of roots by runtime ID, equal on every client because the calls are); (ii) the frame
     hooks: the script RNG's MD frame scopes already see each MD frame and its cue on the frame thread (lockstep_script_rng_md.h,
     used by lockstep_guidance_local.h): enter scope k at a slot-k frame, leave at its end, keep it across nested frames of
     the same slot, refuse (stop) a nested frame of another slot's tree; (iii) P_k as an MD value: MD stores player.entity
     as a runtime ID ($Player = player.entity) and resolves it with Find(map, id, 4) later: P_k needs a runtime ID in the
     registry, the same on every client (today P_k is built by the factory and never connected [F lockstep_pilot_slots.h];
     registered: reg1, 8 Nov, x4ls play with 2 players, gate.pilot_slots_registry every 600 ticks: P_2 has runtime ID 1615fa
     on both clients and Find(map, id, 4) answers P_2 itself at 600..3600 on both [F]); (iv) events on P_k: listeners such as event_object_changed_sector
     object="player.entity" registered in scope k bind to P_k, which nothing moves in the component tree: slot k's sector
     changes must be posted on P_k by our seat code [I]. WRONG for a seated slot (11 Nov, section 13): X4 posts changed
     zone on a seated P_k itself.
  C2 Per slot managers. A mission manager per slot (0x3d0 bytes, ctor 0x1137710) and a conversation manager per slot,
     their pointers (0x6cf1658, 0x38ee230) swapped with the slot scope (MD's create_offer/create_mission/start_conversation
     inside slot k's frames land in slot k's) and with client k's presentation scope (client k's HUD, map, briefing and
     dialog read slot k's) [I: the UI readers must run inside the presentation scope; most are UI slot 6/10]. The
     conversation manager's update (0x1177d60) once per slot in its scope. Saves: out of scope until session saves exist.
  C3 Rewards and reputation. Factions mode: g_PlayerFaction (0x38f4bd0) = slot k's faction (x4lan_playerk) inside slot k's
     frames: reward_player money, faction.player relations, ownership of rewarded objects. Coop mode (every slot `player`):
     a personal wallet needs an account per slot (FactionClass +0x280 is the faction's account [F label]), swapped the same
     way; personal reputation in coop has no carrier at all (relations are per faction) -> a user decision: shared
     reputation in coop, or per slot factions in coop as well.
  C4 Offers per player. GenericMissions.Manager reacts to slot 1's sector changes [F 2f]: per slot generation in scope k
     (signal the manager's EvaluateSectorMissions instance in scope k when slot k changes sector, or our own small MD layer
     that signals each $StaticGMCues entry's GenerateGenericMission with $Sector = slot k's sector, in scope k); the
     generated trees are slot k's (C1 i).
  C5 Slot k's calls in slot k's scope. The replay's Lua-run ops are canonical today; for (c) ops 0x47/0x48/0x49 of slot k
     apply inside pslot scope k on every client (the native ops already do so in path_release); slot k's accept then reaches
     the offer cue of slot k's manager.
  C6 Notifications to slot k's screen (task 3). MD's UI side effects (show_notification, show_help, write_to_logbook,
     speak/add_npc_line, mission HUD updates: Mission*UpdateEvent) are presentation, not world: inside slot k's frames they
     must reach client k's UI and nobody else's. Where they post to the singleton's event source they reach P_k, which only
     client k's presentation scope makes X4's player [I]; where they use global UI queues, a filter on the frame's slot drops
     them on clients j != k [I]. Needs a census of those actions' targets (static, then a live row per action kind).
4b Why one generic mission for slot 2 is not contained now
  It needs C1 (with P_k registered and its events), C2 (a second mission manager, swapped in two scopes, plus the
  conversation manager when the offer comes from a conversation), C5, C6 and for the payout C3: five engine-level pieces,
  each needing its own live check, before the first mission can belong to slot 2. Built now instead: the parts every model
  shares and (c) reuses unchanged: the cue references (C5 carries them), the capture of all mission/conversation entries,
  the guard hole of 2d, the receipt of both managers (C2's per slot managers join it), the MD fixture and scripted ops (the
  same plan, run as slot 2, is (c)'s first live test).
4c The first (c) mission type: GM_Find_Object
  Few player reads (9 [F count]): spawn place around player.sector (Setup_SpawnLocation), find_sector_in_range from
  player.entity, the reward add_inventory entity="player.entity" (P_k's own inventory: the per-player inventory comes for
  free with C1), relation debug reads, player.age; the objective is RML_FindObject (proximity of the player: slot k's under
  C1). One briefing cutscene (lockbox orbit) like 2d. Steps: C1 + C2 (missions only) + C5 for its offer, accept, abort and
  briefing, C6 for its notifications; then C3 for its credits (factions mode first). Live: scripted plan of 7a run with
  client 2 as the actor, plus 'mis_fixture' for slot 2.
4d The path to all generic missions
  After GM_Find_Object: the audit per gm_*.xml of its player.* reads (393 in 37 files; 195 player.entity, 86 player.sector,
  43 player.age, 22 player.ship, 14 player.zone [F counts]) and of its global state (md.$ lists shared by all players, e.g.

5. (d) STORY AND PLOT MISSIONS PER PLAYER (separate work item; what it needs)
  Player-specific state: plot progress in MD globals and in the player entity's blackboard (player.entity.$...): per slot
  under C1 for the blackboard (P_k's), but md.$ globals and static cue states (a plot's cues are static, instantiate=false)
  are one per world -> a per slot copy of a plot means instantiating its cue tree per slot or rewriting it (our own MD).
  Cutscenes: play_cutscene on the player's view (story_*.xml 73, scenario_*.xml 56, x4ep1_*.xml 6, gm_* briefings 97 [F
  counts]): a cutscene of slot k must play on client k only while the world it shows stays identical (cutscene objects on
  every client, the camera on k's) [I]. Teleports and seat changes of the player (teleport_player, set_player_*): slot k's
  seat through the pilot slots' seat code. Plot flags and unlocks shared by the world (faction relations, sector ownership,
  research): world-level consequences of one player's plot reach everyone (a decision per plot).
  Feasible first: plots with little player-specific state and no forced seat changes, e.g. the research and venture
  stories (story_research_*.xml, story_ventures.xml: menus and MD flags), then faction plots (story_paranid, buccaneers),
  last the main/tutorial scenarios (scenario_*.xml, tutorial*.xml: 711 and 518 player.* reads, forced camera and seat).
  Until then: (a) keeps them slot 1's (their UI calls replayed, MD canonical); a per-plot "slot 1 only" flag is enough.
